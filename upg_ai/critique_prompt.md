# Prompt: adversarial review of a PhD upgrade chapter draft

You are reviewing the draft introduction to a PhD upgrade chapter, attached below. Treat this as a mock viva by a sceptical analytic philosopher. The author does not want reassurance, polish suggestions, or a rewrite. The author wants to know what will fail in one to two years' time, when the promissory notes in this draft have to be cashed into full chapters.

## What the project is

This is a practice-based art PhD (arts and humanities department), but the chapter under review must stand as narrowly defined philosophy. The central thesis, called Q2 in the draft, is a normative conditional: **thick human values — the full complexity of our moral and cultural inheritance — need not persist for a future to count as good, when that future is evaluated under a constraint of maximal impartiality (no arbitrary privileging of person, place, time, or species).** The thesis is explicitly not a prediction about what values future AI systems will have (that predictive question, Q1, is bracketed), not a claim that value-loading is permissible or impermissible (Q3, mostly bracketed, with one working commitment: any value-loading should take the form of "thin" open-future side-constraints in Feinberg's sense — non-production of suffering, resource satiability, and similar — rather than a comprehensive doctrine), and not a claim about the feasibility of training methods (Q4, bracketed).

The method is a thought experiment: a Bracewell–von Neumann probe — a solitary, self-repairing AI on a millennia-long interstellar voyage, chosen to maximize inferential distance from human forms of life while staying within physics — plus variants (probe swarms, Dyson-scale entities) treated as Dennettian knobs. The reader is asked not to *occupy* an impartial standpoint (conceded to be an unachievable posture, per Williams and Nagel) but to *apply a criterion*: identify what is lost in such a world, and test which candidate losses can still be stated once the human vantage point is set aside. The validity of the criterion itself is to be defended in a later chapter by a selective evolutionary debunking argument (Street; de Lazari-Radek & Singer). The scenario will be presented in two literary registers ("warm" and "cold") with identical stipulations, as a control for the framing effects the draft criticizes in the paperclip-maximizer thought experiment.

## What is fixed and what is movable

Fixed — do not propose abandoning these:
- The thesis shape: a future without full complex human values can be plausibly good by some impartial standard.
- A thought-experiment framing, roughly of the kind described.
- The conditional structure (the thesis holds under the criterion; flat denial of the criterion is addressed separately by the debunking track).

Movable — criticism and alternatives welcome here:
- The specific scenario (is the BvNP the right vehicle? does a solitary probe smuggle in favourable knob-settings?).
- The specific formulation of the criterion and the relationship between "impartial evaluation," Rawlsian devices, Sidgwick's point of view of the universe, and Bostrom's Cosmic Host.
- The taxonomy of questions (Q1–Q4) and objections.
- The thin/thick value distinction's exact content and the specific thin set.
- The argumentative route from scenario to thesis.
- Everything about the art-practice elements: these are the flexible, dependent variable of the project. Do not spend effort critiquing them.

## Your tasks, in order

**Task 1 — Independent demolition attempt.** Before reading Part B below, list the most serious philosophical problems you can find. For each: (i) state the problem precisely; (ii) rate severity (would an examiner consider it embarrassing / substantive / cosmetic); (iii) rate tractability (fixable with a paragraph, fixable with a chapter, or plausibly intractable — i.e. the kind of thing that looks fine in an introduction and collapses when the supporting chapter is actually written); (iv) if the problem is a known move in the literature, name the source an examiner would cite. Prioritize intractability over quantity: three problems that would sink a chapter are worth more than ten quibbles.

Questions worth asking yourself while you look: Does the "criterion, not posture" move actually escape the Williams objection, or just relocate it? Can a selective debunking argument coherently spare the impartiality criterion and non-production of suffering while undermining thick-value intuitions, or does it prove too much or too little? Is "what is lost, stated without the human vantage" a well-formed test, or does every evaluative predicate carry the vantage with it? Does the thin set secretly contain thick content? Does the argument equivocate between "thick values need not persist" and "nothing of value is lost"? Is there a suppressed premise connecting "relational values lack objects in this environment" to any axiological conclusion?

**Task 2 — Alternative routes with the same shape.** Propose one or two substantially different ways to argue the fixed thesis using a thought-experiment framing — different scenario, different criterion formulation, or different argumentative route — and say specifically what each alternative would fix that the current draft cannot fix by patching. If you think the current route is the strongest available, say so and say why; do not invent variety for its own sake.

**Task 3 — The single strongest objection.** Steelman the one objection you would raise if you could raise only one, in the voice of a hostile examiner, at full strength, including the follow-up you would ask after the author's most likely reply.

**Task 4 — Missing interlocutors.** Name any philosopher, paper, or standard objection that this territory obviously touches and the draft does not engage, where the omission itself would look like a gap. Only include genuinely load-bearing omissions, not literature-review completism.

## Part B — already-known issues (read only after completing Task 1)

To avoid duplicated effort, the following are already on the author's list; do not spend your answer restating them unless you think their severity is being underestimated: the debunking chapter does not yet exist and its selectivity is recognized as the most attackable joint; the warm/cold framings must be at strict stipulative parity (a current draft violates this via an experiencelessness stipulation); the relationality claim must be scoped to the solitary case since multipolar variants reintroduce cooperative structure; the relationship between the unoccupied impartial criterion and Bostrom's occupied Cosmic Host is an unresolved decision; the parent-child analogy is acknowledged as disanalogous; predictive vocabulary must not stand in for the normative conditional; the thesis must be distinguished from Sutton/Hanson/Barnett-style successionism.

## Style instructions

Be blunt and specific. No praise, no hedging boilerplate, no summary of the draft back to the author. If a criticism depends on an interpretation of the draft, quote the sentence you are interpreting. If you find nothing intractable, say so plainly rather than manufacturing objections — a clean bill on Task 1 with a strong Task 3 is a useful result.
