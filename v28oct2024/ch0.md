## AI as religion: historical context

The Jewish context is somewhat different, from perspective of theology and eschatology, as is the Islamic. That a type of apocalyptic thinking should emerge in the birthplace, and effective nursery of AI, the United States (particularly California's Bay Area), is not hugely surprising.^[Note that there is a discursively separate thread, not the topic of this chapter, followed in books like [Work Pray Code](https://matrix.berkeley.edu/research-article/work-pray-code-when-work-becomes-religion-in-silicon-valley/) that look at a phenomenon, common in Silicon Valley but a feature perhaps of white-collar jobs more generally, to find transcendent meaning in their jobs (often through structured or organised programs or internal messaging from the companies where the work). At the meta level of how knowledge is formed, ideas have to come from somewhere, and given that conversations around AI, at least initially, come from a largely UK/European/American milieu, it shouldn't be surprising that certain foundational parts of that milieu (e.g. ideas derived from Abrahamic faiths) find their way into conversations. Yet the historical record seems to point at some very specific sedimentation of ideas that support the AI discourse's theological resonances. How can we approach this inheritance? 

### Historical background

#### Scriptural antecedents

X-risk, or extinction, studies are, to some extent, a contemporary iteration of human (specifically Western) eschatalogical concerns, albeit with considerable evolution from the Biblical context, as [Moynihan 2020] documents. Eschaton, deriving from ἔσχατος (or the "end" or "last") is associated semantically and through usage with: τὸ κατέχον ("the one who restrains"), τέλος ("end/purpose/goal"), πλήρωμα ("fullness or completeness" albeit in a slightly different, specifically Christian sense than the philosophical connotation of "plenitude"), and finally παρουσία (the culmination of history, and in Christian eschatology, the second coming of the divine into history). From this lineage, we consider the katechon in more detail, as a key element of teleological eschatology.

#### The argument from modernity

Moynihan, as well as [Sacasas 2023](https://theconvivialsociety.substack.com/p/apocalyptic-ai), use modernity^[*If we keep this section, would be nice to explain what modernity is*] as a frame^[Albeit in different ways, in keeping with their separate projects - Sacasas seems more interested in the condition of modernity being eclipsed.], something that starts with Schmitt, who we recall as the somewhat notorious jurist of the Third Reich. Schmitt, despite this inconvenient history, continues to be cited up in contemporary discourse (right- and left-coded).^[Schmitt is interesting in this context, not least because his book *Political Theology* and its ideas are taken up by elements of Silicon Valley's right-leaning consensus, e.g. Peter Thiel. > All significant concepts of the modern theory of the state are secularized theological concepts not only because of their historical development - in which they were transferred from theology to the theory of the state, whereby, for example, the omnipotent God became the omnipotent lawgiver-but also because of their systematic structure, the recognition of which is necessary for a sociological consideration of these concepts.

That is, as religion took a back seat to secular life, at least in parts of the West, certain aspects of religious thought were incorporated into the statal infratsructure.

#### From failure to immanence

Aside from modernity-as-religiosity-rebottled, a more intriguing perspective Moynihan cites is that it is precisely the *failure* of the Christian promise -- that is, the non-arrival of imminent revelation, the ever deferred unveiling of truth -- after months, then tens, then hundreds of years, that forced Christian meaning-makers, such as the Medieval theologians, to confront the possibility that the Apocalypse may not come anytime soon. 

Eventually this line led to the possibility that the community of believers might be responsible for their own fate, that it would not be dictated from above, a model of progress developed in [Blumenberg 1964](https://academic.oup.com/cornell-scholarship-online/book/38214/chapter-abstract/333133782?redirectedFrom=fulltext). From this root would grow the *immanentising of the eschaton* (see below); it also left behind, according to the reading of Blumenberg in [Moynihan 2023] certain *conceptual desires* for explication, a dissatisfaction with [unknowingness, mystery, and lacunae], that were after all the handmaidens of pre-philosophical ignorance.^[*Cite from Helen de Cruz on wonder and awe*] ^[In the context of AI and x-risk, there is arguably another root: the realisation that humans may be only advanced civilisation within the easily visible parts of the cosmos; this is covered below.]

#### Millenarian desiderata: explication, teleology, and perfectability

What might some of these conceptual desires, or perhaps tendencies, be? Besides a desire and expectation of knowledge and explication, One might think of teleology and perfectability:

- *teleology* in this context means an explanation for the events of the world by reference to their goals or purposes, rather than merely through causes, something that can be seen if one's world-view is conditioned upon a purposeful creation (as both Jewish and Christian thought propose). ([Gray 2016])
- as [Moynihan 2023] explains, the Greek *telos* has neighbours *teleios* and *teleute*, respectively the noun for "perfection" and the verb to "finish" or "die". In a philological-etymological sense, it is easy to see how one might form the view that the direction of progress, whether historical or humans, might be (or ought to be) towards some sort of perfection, with the process culminating in an end of sorts.

It is this tripartite conjunction: of purposeful direction (and implicit inevitability); of perfection via some historical process; and termination of that process (conditional on perfection), that inform the Christian faith in particular, and (the allegation goes) fears about x-risks from AI.^[*Read and cite geraci book 'apocalyptic AI' referenced in vox, and this book from [Megan O'Gyeblin](https://www.amazon.com/God-Human-Animal-Machine-Technology/dp/0525562710/ref=tmm_pap_swatch_0?_encoding=UTF8&qid=&sr=) which showed up in jack clark research, and moynihan cites this [academic study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00146-020-00968-2)*] As Moynihan eloquently puts this crux:

> "It is, by now, trite to point out the resemblance of such belief to age-old apocalyptic and millenarian patterns of thought. But many cultures exhibit such strains. What remains hidden is a deeper, and more specifically Christian, consonance of convictions. Namely, that history is intelligible as the perfecting of mind or spirit, that this perfection is somehow inevitable or imminent, and that arriving at this threshold may precipitate the endangerment of everything meaningfully human." ([Moynihan 2023])

### Mapping the allegation to the (AI) discourse

#### Extinction

Lastly, and here we deviate from the religious narrative, the development of advanced AI does according to some^[One, rather non-intuitive but influential, risk scenario will be discussed in [Chapter...].], *in and of itself* presents catastrophic or x-risks.

## Is religiosity manifested in AI alignment discourse?

Having established at least modest and general intellectual connections between the history of AI alignment fears and certain strains of theological thought, we now drill down into the specific case of Calvinism in the cultural milieu around AI.

#### Immanentising the eschaton

As touched upon above, a rather intriguing puzzle/lacuna of the Early Church was the question of why such a [vividly/luridly]-described future, that of revelation and transcendence, had not come about in the promised time. While Schmitt used this katechnoical deferral to ground his theory of political theology^[*Citation*], another writer, Eric Voegelin side-stepped the katechon's problematic^[*Check that he doesn't say precisely the opposite or whatever in his book*] by suggesting that the inherent, deeply-buried ills of the world (he was starting from a foundation of historical Gnosticism^[*Fn to explain what this is -- note references with john gray/meghan o'gieblyn and pkd*]) might be cured, in the absence of heavenly salvation, by hastening or "immanentising the eschaton" ([Voegelin 1952]), a rather peculiar phrase that turns out to be not uncommon, particularly in twentieth century American conservative Christian [writing](https://pandrewsandlin.substack.com/p/immanentizing-the-eschaton), often to criticise social justice movements.^[*There feels like quite a deep connection to Schmitt, how do we nail that down - see p. 100 of leviathan? Immanent is also big part of political theology.*] 

To make immanent in this context means to find reasons or solutions within our world, rather than relying on some external (or transcendent) force, entity, or explanation. 

It is tempting (in the context of AI alignment) to point to AI doomers (those worried about x-risk from AI) as a katechonic force, holding back the societal disorder that might result from TAI. Or one could point at the likes of Sam Altman or Leopold Aschenbrenner who enthusiastically welcome U.S. government involvement in AGI development, as Schmittian figures pursuing the "one ring to rule them all" strategy. But let us defer, or suspend, that impulse, and instead, continue the search for an Abrahamic inheritance.^[*This can probably be more fully treated in alignment chapter and then linked back, would give the project more cohesion*]

##### Calvinism as a special case

It would be a somewhat cheap shot to point at LessWrong discussions around simulations, Roko's Basilisk, and cast them as major evidence of Abrahamic inheritance, since they could also be seen as incidental references to Buddhist^[*Find examples of this -- this needs to be developed or deleted*] notions. However, the idea of achieving a type of perfection within this world might be less ridiculous, based on the economic and medical promise of AI. More speculatively, as I discuss below, a significant strand of reasoning about how near-future AI systems would make decisions uses so-called "acausal decision theories", which have unexpected theological resonances.^[Acausal decision theory involves making predictions about rational agents’ actions in scenarios where one party’s choices don’t directly affect the other’s through causal pathways. Instead, each party’s actions serve as evidence of how similar agents might act, creating a sort of circular reasoning.]

A specific paradox in acausal decision theory, Newcomb's Problem, which is often discussed in AI-adjacent spaces, seems similar to or isomorphic to a core insight of Calvinism: that how one behaves in this Earthly world is *evidence* of whether one has been already selected by providence as being amongst the elect (and therefore predestined to salvation and heaven).^["Isomorphic" means "has the same shape"; the isomorphism between Calvinist ideas and acausal theory are explored in ([G-W 2023] or [crmflynn 2015](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/4RBnbSgAZmvpPHxfE/newcomb-bostrom-calvin-credence-and-the-strange-path-to-a)] 

The tension between free will and predestination is of course central to Christianity, but Catholicism, through a series of theological maneouvres, starting with St. Augustine onwards the Council of Trent, leaves space for one to freely refuse the salvation that God has granted to all.^[*Check john gray book, i think that might have some stuff on free will*] By contrast, in Calvinism the weight is firmly in favour of predestination, with limited or no ability for one to pass between the damned to the elect (or vice versa). Providence's election is *unconditional* on how one behaves in this life, leading to the question posed in [Stearns and Brawner, p. 30] (cited in [LW the puritans]): 
>'"if election is unconditional and grace is irresistible, then why not sit back and wait for the inevitable?' 

Behaviour is one piece of evidence as to one's elect-status; another is the actual success a person finds in this world.^[Find a good source on Calvinist and Puritan^[*Clarify which we're talking about e.g. this [LW post on Puritans](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/2eJPcPoptvP5chjd3/the-puritans-would-one-box-evidential-decision-theory-in-the)] thought - maybe this source from LW (Stearns and Brawner, “New England Church ‘Relations’ and Continuity in Early Congregational History”, 30), also (Griswold 1934, p. 476) but Ahmed's book should do*] It is easy to see how people armed with such a view would^[See Gerhard Lenski's 1961 study, *The Religious Factor : A Sociological Study of Religion's Impact on Politics, Economics, and Family Life.* that traces the impact of Max Weber's ideas (from *The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism*) on American Protestant business culture.], in a sense, see their worldly success as deserved, or at least powerful evidence of elect-status. Notably, this is not incompatible with them undertaking charitable or altruistic tasks, or being a "good" person (under many broad definitions of the term).^[*If you go there, need to cite the moral philosophy literature*]

As [Stearns and Brawner] write^[*Check source and citation*]:

> "The simplest and perhaps most satisfying method of dealing with this question was that of examining closely personal experience for signs of election. If an anxious Christian could find assurance that the seeds of regeneration had been planted in him, he could then be urged to labor with the Holy Ghost to bring forth the fruits of sanctification and thus participate actively in the work of his own redemption. But the spiritual physicians could not administer real comfort unless they could demonstrate convincingly that the symptoms of true faith and repentance were distinguishable from the simulacra produced by hope and fear."

I will discuss Newcomb's Problem, acausal decision theory, and simulation arguments in future chapters, but the point here is to flag this similarity. Briefly however, Calvinist/Puritan ideas about "how ought one to behave conditional upon electedness" has some similarity to questions about the behaviour certain rational agents should have conditional on some overarchingly transformative event (e.g. the agent finding out that they are in a simulation, or the singularity actually arriving).^[*Find citations*]

## Why does this matter?

We have digressed at some length into the hermeneutics of the Early Church, but hopefully productively: Virno, Schmitt, Agamben, even Wittgenstein, show us different views on the relationship of human to state and to language. Yet they all, at some level, have as their atomic units of value, the human or a human collective. In Agamben and Virno's case, their perspective is liberatory for humans, while Schmitt's values [sit at the preference for stability that isn't a surprising inheritance of the Germany of [1910-1950]].^[*We can do better on the Schmitt thing - he's obviously christian so it can be tied into the perfectability/etc. in this chapter*]

The conceptual handrails that these writers retain (essentialist axioms about human nature) are, to some extent, comforting. But what if we were to jettison them, and look at the human as merely another machinic process, that happens to operate on squishy stuff, but is fundamentally governed by physical and mathematical rules (even if we can't fully write them down just yet). What values would be justifiable in such a situation? This is (one possible version of) the point from where Nick Land started, thereby dismantling as well as re-assembling, *Frankenstein*-style, much of the philosophical infrastructure the other writers based themselves upon. This is also the seam philosophers like [Negarestani 2018] and [Wolfendale forthcoming] mine.

In any case, what are the stakes here: why is it particularly important whether some subset of AI researchers and AI alignment researchers have a conscious or unconscious religiosity?

More concretely, while we haven't found any "smoking gun" pointing to widespread, explicit, Abrahamic religiosity (which would have been a difficult thing to prove without a substantial sociological study), there does seem to be a cultural milieu, skewed towards Judeo-Christian ideas and references, that pervades American society, and seems to exist within both the cultures of technology and AI alignment. What is important or problematic about this?^[*Put this in context of findings by [ember 2022](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/a2XaDeadFe6eHfDwG/the-credibility-of-apocalyptic-claims-a-critique-of-techno) which pretty much addresses this question and references geraci - get jstor access*]

### Present-day harms

The most common problem writers identify with the eschatological preoccupation of AI risk community (whether or not this preoccupation stems from religious roots in any attributable way) is that it might intentionally or otherwise distract from or cause present-day harms to be ignored ([Lanier 2011], [Lovely 2024](https://jacobin.com/2024/01/can-humanity-survive-ai), [Benjamin 2024], [Gebru 2024]). This is a reasonable critique, but is not the focus of this project.

### Weak priors and lock-in

More relevant for our purposes is the possibility that certain, particularly Abrahamic, faiths tend to posit a clear separation of good and evil, which can be seen (albeit in proto-Abrahamic guise) in Zoroastrianism's battling forces *Ahriman* and *Ahura-Mazda*. This separation, which would later be cast under Gnosticism, most obviously shows up in Christianity, and is often referred to as Manichean.^[*Look at john gray's book referred to [in book review here](https://www.bostonreview.net/articles/meghan-ogieblyn-god-might-be/) for more on gnosticism...also PKD article from daniel on manichean vs valentinian.*] As a reviewer [O'Gieblyn 2016](https://www.bostonreview.net/articles/meghan-ogieblyn-god-might-be/) writes of John Gray's position^[*Replace this with something from his book*]: 

>"Gray believes that Gnosticism in its modern incarnation can be traced back to the Enlightenment, which left us with a paradoxical inheritance. On the one hand, the scientific revolution dispelled the idea that man existed at the center of creation. On the other, it transformed the human agent into a subject of empirical study, a kind of technology to be bettered and perfected, opening up the potential for humanity to become godlike."

To the extent this grounds (unconsciously, but also through anecdote, analogy, and metaphor) our expectations and fears of TAI, it will either be a prescient and wise warning, or will put us, long before powerful AIs are on the scene, in an adversarial position *vis a vis* them. It isn't clear whether this is likely to increase civilisational risks, in the manner of self-fulfilling prophecies^[*Look at perils of prediction, isn't this a popperian thing?*], or it is sensible caution. But, *prima facie* woolly conceptions of the divine hoisted up from two thousand years of evolution in some arbitrary (or at least highly contingent) group of fitfully self-aware primates seems like an odd foundation upon which to "align" a putative superintelligence.^[*It would be nice to have some backing for this, or analysis*]

A related issue, touched on by [Moynihan 2023], is our simple epistemic and moral ignorance - we know very little of what the future might look like, and to over-index on our current predictions, particularly under the pressure of strategic and commercial imperatives, seems unwise and might achieve precisely the "lock-in" that longtermist authors suggest is a major risk facing us ([Macaskill 2022], [Ord 2020]).

### Manicheanism and geopolitics

Most concretely, and dangerously, the Manichean good/evil binary seems to start the current leaders (companies and governments that are mostly U.S.-aligned) in AI on an adversarial course with respect to technological and strategic peers (such as China). This complicates the task of finding cooperative equilibria or compromises that would help avoid the worst, extinction-level outcomes.^[*Find sources, probalby will have done this earlier in aschenbrenner/etc*]

Such a "race dynamic" has long been predicted ([Bostrom 2014], [Chakrabarti 2022]). In practice, it seems like there has been some progress in [Track II](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/laying-the-groundwork-for-us-china-ai-dialogue/) dialogues between academics in the U.S. and China, that try to identify points of cooperation and clarify different understandings of what the relevant problems actually are. The 2023 Bletchley Declaration (which included the U.S. and China) on AI is another example of dialogue. On the other hand, evidence of race dynamics can be seen in the 2024 [Situational Awareness](https://situational-awareness.ai/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/situationalawareness.pdf) document by ex-OpenAI staffer Leopold Aschenbrenner which sets out a stark vision of global military and economic competition.^[*Make the point that this is usg influential e.g. ivanka trump circulated it*] ^[OpenAI's Sam Altman is equally blunt: 'There is no third option — and it’s time to decide which path to take. The United States currently has a lead in AI development, but continued leadership is far from guaranteed. Authoritarian governments the world over are willing to spend enormous amounts of money
to catch up and ultimately overtake us. Russian dictator Vladimir Putin has darkly warned that the country that wins the AI race will “become the ruler of the world,” and the People’s Republic of China has said that it aims to become the global leader in AI by 2030.' ([Leahy 2024])] Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei takes a similarly adversarial view in what he calls an "entente strategy" described as 
>"a coalition of democracies seeks to gain a clear advantage (even just a temporary one) on powerful AI by securing its supply chain, scaling quickly, and blocking or delaying adversaries’ access to key resources like chips and semiconductor equipment." ([Amodei 2024](https://darioamodei.com/machines-of-loving-grace#4-peace-and-governance))

None of these give much hope that the path to TAI or AGI will be unaffected by great power competition.^[*I think we can do a better job telling this story with some quotes*]
